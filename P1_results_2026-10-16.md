# P1 results: QGLP on Nifty 50 members, 2011-2025 (run 16-Oct-2026)

Code: `code/s08_analysis.py` on the ProwessDX panel (`code/s07_panel_prowess.py`). Data is licensed CMIE ProwessDX: do not publish.

## Design as run
- 15 rebalances (30 June, 2011-2025), 50 members each, point-in-time fundamentals (results dates from Prowess).
- Equal-weight quintiles on the QGLP composite, held 12 months, re-formed each June. 180 holding months (Jul 2011 - Jun 2026).
- Benchmark: cap-weighted member proxy (weights = market cap at rebalance). Not the official Nifty 50 TRI.
- Alpha: IIMA four-factor (MKT-RF, SMB, HML, WML) to Dec 2025, Newey-West with 12 lags (matches 12-month overlap).

## Benchmark check
Proxy vs NIFTY50_PRICE: monthly correlation 0.99; proxy runs 1.27% a year higher (dividends, as expected).

## Findings
| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Composite quintiles | No monotone pattern. Q5 (highest QGLP) returns 6.1% a year excess vs 9.6% for Q1. Sharpe 0.30 vs 0.43 in Q1, 0.43 for benchmark. |
| Long-short Q5 minus Q1 | -3.6% a year, Sharpe -0.28. Excluding 2020 it is -4.9%. |
| 4-factor alpha, Q5 minus Q1 | -1.9% a year, t = -0.58, p = 0.56 |
| Fama-MacBeth: Q (quality) | slope -0.20% per month, t = -2.19 |
| Fama-MacBeth: L (long-run ROCE) | slope -0.19% per month, t = -3.02 |
| Fama-MacBeth: G (EPS growth), P (earnings yield) | not significant |
| Fama-MacBeth: composite | -0.20% per month, t = -1.24 (not significant) |
| Joint regression with size and momentum | no QGLP component significant on its own |
| Bootstrap: Q5 Sharpe vs benchmark | -0.04, 95% CI [-0.11, 0.04], p = 0.31 |
| Holm across the family | Only the L signal survives (adjusted p = 0.018) |

## Reading
- The QGLP hypothesis is not supported in this sample. Where anything is significant, it is the reverse: the more profitable and long-run-high-ROCE names underperformed.
- Only the L signal survives multiple-testing correction, and the composite does not. That fits the components pulling in opposite directions.
- The result is not a 2020 artefact.

## Limitations (must be stated with any result)
1. **Only 15 independent cross-sections.** Each signal is observed once a year, so power is low even with 180 monthly observations.
2. **Benchmark is a proxy** until LSEG gives the official Nifty 50 TRI. The proxy only affects the Sharpe test, not the factor alphas or Fama-MacBeth.
3. **Survivorship.** Members that merged or were delisted (HDFC Ltd, Reliance Capital, IDFC, JP Associates, Ranbaxy, Sterlite, Cairn) have no returns after their exit date and drop out of the averages, instead of showing their final return. This biases the results and needs a delisting-return adjustment before publication.
4. **Factors end Dec 2025.** The last six holding months of the 2025 cohort are not in the alpha regressions.
5. **Insurers** (HDFC Life, SBI Life) have G and P only from 2022. Their Q and L are blank.
6. **Earnings timing.** Results dates come from Prowess board-meeting records, so the point-in-time cutoff is actual disclosure, not an assumed 60-day lag.
7. **Returns are NSE-based total returns from Prowess.** Dividends are included; the NSE series is used for every stock.

## Final benchmark and survivorship (17-Oct-2026)
**Benchmark.** Equal-weight average of the monthly returns of two AMFI-listed Nifty 50 index funds, UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund (regular growth, code 100822) and SBI Nifty Index Fund (regular growth, code 102272) (`code/s12_benchmark_avg.py`). The two funds correlate at 0.9994 monthly, and their average return is 11.4% a year over the sample. Both are net of their own fees, so the benchmark runs a little below the official Nifty 50 TRI. It is a proxy, not the official index; the official TRI needs licensed access (LSEG), which was not available.

**Sharpe test vs this benchmark** (`s10`, 5,000 block-bootstrap runs):
- Top quintile Sharpe minus benchmark: −0.02 (p = 0.55), 95% interval −0.10 to +0.06.
- Bottom quintile minus benchmark: +0.02 (p = 0.64).
- The top-minus-bottom spread does not use the benchmark, so it is unchanged: p = 0.31.

**Survivorship bias.** The membership is point-in-time: each year's portfolio holds that year's 50 members. But the returns of companies that later left the index are not complete, so the results are biased. Seven members have no returns after they exit:
- HDFC Ltd (merged into HDFC Bank, 2023)
- Reliance Capital (delisted 2025)
- IDFC (merged 2024)
- JP Associates (delisted 2026)
- Ranbaxy (acquired 2015)
- Sterlite Industries (merged into Vedanta 2013)
- Cairn India (acquired 2017)

The data source ends their series at the last traded price, so their losses or merger outcomes are excluded from the later holding months. Two consequences:
1. **The quintile portfolios are biased.** Companies that collapsed or were absorbed on poor terms drop out of the averages, so the portfolios look better than they were. The bias is not known in direction for every signal, because the excluded companies were both high- and low-scoring.
2. **The benchmark is not affected in the same way.** Index-fund NAVs record every exit at its actual outcome, so the benchmark includes the losses that the portfolios drop. That makes the benchmark comparison slightly unfavourable to the portfolios.

The bias was accepted by the user as a known limitation. It needs an exit-return adjustment (the delisting price, or the merger ratio) before any publication.
